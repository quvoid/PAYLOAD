§ 1

Two audiences, one structure

Search engines want topical silos, crawlable hub-and-spoke linking, unique content per URL, and valid structured data. Answer engines want extractable atomic facts, answer-first prose, question-shaped headings, stable anchors, and honest freshness. These overlap almost completely — and this product is unusually well-suited to both.

The reason is the pipeline. A sentence like "23% of 1,050 reviews mention bloating (Amazon.in 847, Flipkart 203), concentrated in the unflavoured variant" is a computed fact with named sources. That is exactly what an answer engine quotes and links back to. The format is an AEO asset and you get it for free — see §11.

§ 2

Silos and URLs

A silo works when link flow follows topic. It breaks when everything links to everything — which is what happens by default the moment editors add links by hand. So the rules below are enforced in the CMS, and the diagram shows both the links that get generated and the one that gets refused.

Flat product URLs, silo carried by links

Nesting products under their category makes the silo visible in the URL, but every recategorisation becomes a redirect and a dual-category product has no clean home. Internal linking is a considerably stronger topical signal than URL path segments — and it's the one you can change without a redirect. So: flat /reviews/[product], silo expressed through breadcrumbs, BreadcrumbList, and generated links. The /reviews/ prefix also puts the search intent in the path, matching the real query shape.

/best/[query] is the highest-leverage page type you have. It matches "best X" queries head-on, it's the ordered-ItemList shape answer engines reach for, and it's nearly free — the ranking already exists in your scored catalogue. Treat it as a first-class collection that regenerates when scores move, not a report someone writes.
§ 3

Entity relations

The site should read as a small knowledge graph rather than a pile of pages. That means brands, aspects and authors are real collections with typed relations — not strings in a text field on the product.

§ 4

§ 5

Semantic HTML and citable anchors

The static output has no excuse for div soup. Every region is a real element with a real label, and every claim has a stable id so an answer engine can deep-link the fact rather than the page.

§ 7

Crawl control

A review site with filters is a crawl-trap generator. The cleanest fix isn't a directive — it's making the bad URLs impossible to exist in the first place.

Tags need a promotion rule or they will dilute the site. Most tags stay noindex filter helpers. A tag becomes an indexable Collection only when an editor has written unique intro copy and it holds ≥8 published products. The CMS enforces the threshold — it will not let anyone tick "index" on a tag with four products and no copy. Sitemaps split by page type, each with accurate lastmod, so an indexing problem is diagnosable per type instead of one opaque number.
§ 8

The CMS

Payload CMS 3 inside the Next.js app, Postgres via Drizzle. What changes in v4 is its job: the CMS is now the enforcement layer for the information architecture, not a catalogue editor with SEO fields bolted on.

§ 9

The publish gate

You already had an approval gate for the verdict. This extends it with machine checks that run before an editor looks — so nobody spends time reviewing a page that can't ship — and re-assert at the moment of publishing.

§ 11

Pipeline and providers

Six stages, triggered from the admin. Resolution and classification are human input, which is why this is the part that got dramatically cheaper to build. The CategoryPack parameterises three of what remains.

Keys are leased from a credential broker with per-provider circuit breakers; every adapter output is Zod-validated with a golden-file test in CI; raw payloads go to R2 so re-parses are free. Both providers are async — the workflow step suspends and a webhook wakes it.

§ 12

Freshness

A curated catalogue rots silently, and staleness is now both a data-quality problem and a ranking problem. Answer engines discount undated commercial content, correctly.

§ 13

Cost, bottleneck, build order

Spend is predictable — it happens when an editor presses a button, not when traffic arrives. Which means the constraint sits somewhere less obvious.

Build order

SEO structure is not a later phase. Silos, entities, schema and the publish gate must exist before the first page is indexed — retrofitting an IA onto 500 published pages means 500 redirects and a ranking dip you didn't need.

PHASE 0

Spike

3–4 days

One product end to end: script → drafted verdict → one hand-built page with full semantic markup and JSON-LD. Validate the schema, then answer honestly whether the verdict beats reading the star rating.

PHASE 1

Operator loop, structurally correct

3–4 weeks

Payload with Product, Category, Brand, Author · resolution assist · bulk import · both providers · full-fetch workflow · sampled extraction · rules verdict · publish gate with structural checks · semantic page with JSON-LD · silo hubs with intro copy · generated breadcrumbs and links · split sitemaps · slug locking and auto-redirects. Two divergent packs.

PHASE 2

Entity depth

2–3 weeks

Aspect topic hubs · Collection best-of pages generated from scores · Comparison pages · link graph screen with orphan and depth reporting · SEO queue · analysis versioning · fake-review scoring · Hinglish · dedupe · tag promotion rule.

PHASE 3

AEO and freshness

2 weeks

FAQ generation and validation · answer-first enforcement · markdown mirrors · llms.txt · scheduled refresh with the dateModified discipline · price fast lane · stale queue · auto-republish · methodology, sources and author pages.

PHASE 4

Growth

ongoing

Affiliate links with disclosure · price-drop alerts · saved comparisons · more packs and silos · editor roles and audit log.

What actually earns citations here is honesty. "Not enough data to judge" and "4.3★ raw versus 3.9★ credibility-weighted" are differentiated, verifiable claims that no affiliate-farm competitor will make. Answer engines are getting better at preferring sources that qualify their claims — which makes the honest engineering and the growth strategy the same decision.
